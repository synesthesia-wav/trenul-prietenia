  • An Unsatisfactory rating is FMCSA's finding that the carrier is unfit to operate. The prohibition in 385.13 applies — and it also makes the carrier ineligible for federal contracts.
  • Failure to file an acceptable corrective action plan after a failed safety audit revokes the carrier's USDOT registration. That fleet is gone, and it was your insured.
  • A single Hours-of-Service BASIC above the intervention threshold is enough for FMCSA's inspection-selection algorithm to place the carrier in the "Inspect" group — the priority list roadside inspectors work from.

What your client gets

  • The file — hours-of-service records, supporting documents, driver qualification files, vehicle records, insurance, drug-and-alcohol programme, accident register — assembled in the order FMCSA requests them, with every gap closed or listed.
  • A written correction trail: for every change, the source document that justifies it, and the driver's own certification. The original record is never altered or erased.
  • The deadlines on a page: the 12-month audit date, the 45-or-60-day corrective action plan window, and the daily deadlines that move a carrier onto a priority list.
  • Something you can put in front of your underwriter, and that the carrier can put in front of FMCSA, an adjuster, or its own attorney.
