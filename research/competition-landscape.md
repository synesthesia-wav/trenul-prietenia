# Competition: the real price and service landscape

Written 2026-10-19, during the demand panel. This page is the one that most changes the plan. **Two of the advisors independently found errors in our regulatory citations while researching this market — those corrections have been applied to every document (see `gotchas/evidence-rules-and-known-errors.md`).**

## The finding that matters

**We are not the only people doing this, we are not first, and the market is priced well below what our plan assumed.** A competitor sweep found a dense field of US vendors selling exactly what we planned to sell — HOS log auditing, ELD compliance support, new-entrant audit preparation, DataQ challenges — with published per-driver monthly prices from **$29 to $199**, and one-off audit-prep events from **$249 to $799**.

**The wedge survives the sweep. The price does not.** Nothing found in the sweep sells the thing we actually designed: a per-correction file where **the driver personally certifies each change under 395.30(d)**, every entry carrying its source document, plus a published log of the corrections we **refused** to make. No vendor publishes a refusal log. No vendor's published scope mentions the driver certifying our proposed edits.

So the position is real but narrow, and it is now a **position against a much larger set of substitutes than our price ladder showed**.

### And the two that matter most structurally

- **Sentinel Compliance HQ** is the most dangerous competitor found. At $49/driver/month it includes new entrant audit preparation, ELD compliance support and log auditing, and at $99 it adds a dedicated compliance manager, mock DOT audits and insurance coordination. **Our $130/truck/month is above a competitor whose $99 tier bundles more scope than we offer.** Sentinel also runs a free compliance check as its magnet — **exactly the lead magnet we designed.** It appears to be a small, unfunded, US operation; its scale is not disclosed.
- **Insurance agencies that bundle compliance.** The UIIA intermodal directory lists agencies advertising "insurance, bonds, compliance & IFTA" (Truck Insurance Office, 247 USA Truck Insurance, Truxsurance). **They compete for the same renewal conversation we want**, and they have the carrier relationship already. This is a competitor set, not a channel.

## The wedge check — and it holds

Searched for: any vendor selling a source-documented HOS correction file in which the driver certifies each correction under 395.30(d); any vendor publishing a log of corrections they refused to make.

**Nothing found.** The nearest thing is regulatory language and FMCSA guidance (the "Editing and Annotations" FAQ, which describes exactly our workflow: carrier edits must be annotated, and *the driver must confirm (certify) that any carrier edit is accurate, and resubmit the records*). **That is FMCSA describing our product.** No vendor claims it, and no vendor publishes a refusal log.

**Two consequences:**

1. **The differentiator is real and it is regulatory, not promotional.** We are not competing on service quality. We are the only proposition where the record is defensible *because the driver certified it and the source document is attached* — and the only one where the customer can see what we declined to do.
2. **But it is a narrow moat in a crowded field.** Every other finding from the panel assumes we have a market position. We have a *differentiated method in a commoditised market*, and the pricing power that implies is unproven.

## What this does to the plan

**The price.** Our plan said $130/truck/month defends against the staffed-HOS band and never against the $25–65 automation tier. That was wrong twice over: the automation tier reaches **$199/truck** (My Safe Nest, with mock audits included), and the *specialist* competitors reach $49–99 per driver. **The honest position: $130 is a premium over the automation tier and at or below the full-service tier, and it is not obviously defensible without the driver-certified file as the thing being bought.** Two advisors independently reached the same conclusion from different directions — one from the value equation (a retainer at this price needs an alternative to anchor against, and at this fleet size there is none), one from SPIN (the buyer's reference class is the *event* bracket, and a retainer has no reference class below ~15 trucks).

**The event price.** Our plan set the new-entrant event at $1,500–4,000. **The published event band is $249–799** (NRB $249, ICG $350, Sentinel $399, Motor Carrier HQ $549, The Trucker Consultant $699, Lili $799, DOT Doctor $999). Our price was 2–5x the top of the market. Two advisors independently flagged this from their own research without seeing each other's work.

**The segment.** The new-entrant beachhead is not just churny (56% of programme exits are inactive/no-show) — it is **crowded with incumbents who have done it for a decade** (LBC: 500+ audits, 99.8% pass rate, 11 years). We would be arriving with no track record against vendors with 99.8% pass rates and published prices. **This materially weakens Tier 0 and strengthens the Conditional-rated population**, where the published competitor set is thinner and where the buyer is a renewing insured rather than a cash-poor startup.

**The channel.** Two of our three planned channels (insurance agents, ELD resellers) are, on inspection, populated with people who **already bundle compliance**. The agent is not a neutral referrer for us; he may be our competitor. This does not kill the channel — the file product is narrower than what an agency bundles — but it changes the ask from "refer me a client" to "here is a file you cannot produce in-house," which is a much higher bar to clear.

## Corrections to our own regulatory citations, made during this sweep

Three advisors found the same errors independently. All now fixed, and the corrections matter because our customer-facing scripts quote these provisions:

| Was | Now | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| CAP deadline cited to **385.321** | **385.319(c)(1)** = 60 days, **(c)(2)** = 45 days (passenger, placarded hazmat); enforced by **385.325(b)** (out-of-service effective **day 61** / day 46) and **385.325(c)** (no interstate operation from that date) | 385.321 is the auto-fail table, not the deadline. A buyer who checks the citation finds the wrong section. |
| "one bad log fails you" | **385.321(b) item 13** — 395.8(a) auto-fails at **"51% or more of examined records"**, not on one occurrence (same threshold on 396.17(a), item 16) | Our strongest-sounding claim was a checkable overstatement. The true version is *more* useful as a selling fact: it is a rate, countable, and it converts dread into a measurement. |
| "FMCSA audits every new carrier within 12 months" stated flatly | The subpart D preamble says within 12 months of receiving the USDOT number for property, 120 days for passenger; **385.307** sets the 18-month monitoring period and the audit happens once there are sufficient records, **generally at least 3 months** | A sceptic can disprove the flat version in one click. Same failure mode as the Sisic sentence. |
| — | **385.339 does not exist.** Subpart D runs 385.301–385.337, then Subpart I at 385.601 | It is the section a seller would guess for the corrective action plan. The correct one is 385.319(c). |

**And one gap the panel exposed in our own research:** we had claimed that "missing supporting documentation for the RODS" is one of the 16 automatic-fail regulations. **It is not.** The 16 are 382.x, 383.x, 387.x, 391.x, 395.8(a), 396.9(c)(2), 396.11(a)(3), 396.17(a). The supporting-document list is FMCSA's *Safety Audit Resource Guide*, not the auto-fail table. Our plan's claim that the audit "fails on our exact skill" was overstated — and the correct reading is better for us: the audit is more likely to be failed on drug-and-alcohol, insurance and vehicle-inspection items, which are other people's jobs, while the *daily* paperwork is our product.

## The honest summary

| Claim in the plan | Status after the sweep |
|---|---|
| We are differentiated | **Narrowly true.** Driver-certified source-documented corrections plus a published refusal log is unclaimed. Nothing else in the field is. |
| $130/truck/month is defensible | **Unproven, and probably too high.** A $99/driver tier includes more scope; full-service per-truck plans reach $199. |
| The event price of $1,500–4,000 is right | **Wrong by 2–5x.** The published band is $249–799. |
| The new-entrant beachhead is ours | **No.** It is the most contested segment in the market, with a 10-year incumbent at 99.8% pass rate, and a 56% churn/no-show rate. |
| Insurance agents and ELD resellers are our channel | **Partly a competitor set.** Many already bundle compliance. |
| The $80 offshore price is our real competitor | **No.** The real competitors are US vendors at $25–199, and they speak the buyer's language and are reachable in one timezone. |
| FMCSA publishes the document list free | **Confirmed** — so the *knowledge* is not sellable, only the assembly, the retention discipline and the production readiness. |
