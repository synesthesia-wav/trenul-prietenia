# Interview Guides

Web research cannot answer three things: whether a fleet owner will pay $130 per truck per month, what a request from a driver actually costs us to serve, and what an insurance agent will actually refer. These guides close that gap.

**Rules for the interviewer:**
- **Never pitch.** You are gathering evidence, not selling. Say so in the first thirty seconds and again at the end: *"I'm not selling you anything today. I'm researching a service that might serve you, and I need to know whether it would."*
- **Ask about the last event, not the general practice.** "Walk me through the last time a driver had a problem with his log" produces facts. "How do you handle ELD issues?" produces pleasantries.
- **Ask for dollars.** Every cost question gets a number. "A lot" is not an answer; push once, politely: *"roughly what would that have cost you?"*
- **Do not correct their answers.** If they say the ELD is "fraud-proof," let it stand and note it.
- **Do not teach the regulation.** We are measuring their reality, not our understanding. If they ask what the rule says, answer plainly and mark it as a coaching moment for the sales team, not data.
- **Record verbatim where it is about money or fear.** Their words are our copy.
- **Never share another participant's answers.** If they ask "what do other fleets pay?", say: *"I'm not going to tell you what other fleets told me, but I'll tell you what we charge once we know it fits."*

**Sample sizes and targets:**

| Guide | Who | Target | Primary question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Fleet owner, 5–75 trucks | 10 | Will they pay $130/truck/month for this? |
| B | Truck insurance agent | 3 | Is the broker channel real, and what shape is the deal? |
| C | ELD reseller / installer | 1–2 | Would they white-label, and at what margin? |
| D | Dispatcher / compliance admin | 2 | What is the real request volume and what does it cost to serve? |

Run A and D first, in parallel. B and C after, once the price position is fixed.

## Guide A — Fleet owner

**Who:** owner or GM of a carrier with 5–75 power units, ideally 15–50, ideally with a violation or a claim in the last 24 months. Small fleets (1–4 trucks) are still worth two conversations for contrast, but do not count them toward the target sample.

**How to get them:** the partners' warm network (subject to the prior-employer legal check), insurance-agent introductions, and ELD-reseller introductions. **Do not use FMCSA census cold-calls for this sample** — owners who have been cold-called by a compliance vendor for a decade will give you a rehearsed answer.

**Opening (say almost exactly this):**
> "I'm not selling you anything today. I'm researching a service for small fleets — a desk that answers drivers' log questions, checks the paperwork against the bill of lading, and prepares the corrections — and I need to know whether it would actually be useful before we build it. Can I ask you some questions about how you handle this now? About 20 minutes."

### Part 1 — the last event (the core)

1. "Tell me about the last time one of your drivers had a problem with his log. What happened?"
2. "Who dealt with it? What did they actually do?"
3. "How long did that take, start to finish?"
4. "What did it cost you — in money, in the driver's time, in the office's time?"
5. "What would have happened if nobody had caught it?"

### Part 2 — frequency and volume

6. "How often does that happen? Once a month? Once a week? Per truck, or for the whole fleet?"
7. "Of your trucks, how many have had a log issue in the last 60 days?" *(tests our 6-per-truck-per-month assumption against the owner's experience — they will over- or under-estimate; note the direction.)*
8. "If a driver calls you at 2am on a Sunday because his log won't accept something, what actually happens?"

### Part 3 — the consequence (the money)

9. "Have you had a driver cited for hours of service in the last two years? What did it cost you in fines, lost work, and the argument with the customer?"
10. "Has an insurance company ever raised your premium, or asked about your safety score? Do you know your score?"
11. "What is the most one bad hours-of-service citation has ever cost you?" *(This number anchors the value stack. Get a real figure, not a guess.)*
12. "What did the last FMCSA compliance review or audit cost you — in time and in fees?"

### Part 4 — the trust question (the one that decides the sale)

13. "Would you be comfortable with an outside company reviewing your drivers' records and preparing corrections — with the driver making the actual entry and signing it?" *(We need to know whether the "we don't touch your ELD" reassurance lands or feels like a limitation.)*
14. "If you did hire someone like that, what would you need to see before you trusted them?"
15. "If you did hire someone like that, what would make you fire them?" *(This defines the SLA in the customer's own words. It is usually about communication and honesty, not accuracy.)*
16. "Who else do you know with a similar fleet? Would you be comfortable giving me their name?" *(Referral mechanics — but do not ask for the name in front of the owner if they are hesitant. Ask for "an introduction" instead.)*

### Part 5 — the price (do not skip; do not lead)

17. "What are you paying for compliance today — for the software, for the ELD, for any outside help?" *(Establishes their total spend and what they see as the normal price of this.)*
18. "If a company did all of that — the log questions, the paperwork checks, the correction files, someone on call — what would that be worth to you?" *(Open-ended first, before any number.)*
19. "Would $130 per truck per month for that be a decision you would make, or not?" *(Straight. A yes/no with a reason is worth more than a negotiation.)*
20. "What would that need to include for you to say yes?"

**Close (say this):**
> "Thank you — this is genuinely more useful than what I expected to hear. I will not follow up to sell you anything. If I do come back with something, it will be a specific thing I built from what you told me. Can I send you the paperwork checklist? It's one page."

**Record:** verbatim quotes on cost, trust, and price. The price reaction to $130 *before* any explanation is the single most valuable datum in this guide.

## Guide B — Truck insurance agent or broker

**Who:** an agency or brokerage writing commercial truck lines, ideally 20+ trucking clients, ideally with a loss-prevention or safety-services person you can also speak to. Carriers writing owner-operators heavily are the best target.

**Why:** the agent already has the exact conversation — "your safety score is going to cost you money at renewal" — and already has a book of carriers who cannot be reached by cold call. The agent is also the only buyer in the chain whose *incentive* is reduced claims rather than more premium.

**Opening:**
> "I'm not selling insurance and I'm not selling compliance software. I'm researching whether small fleets would pay for a staffed hours-of-service desk — someone who answers their drivers' log questions and prepares the correction paperwork. I want to know whether that would be useful to your trucking clients, and what it would take for you to be comfortable putting your name next to it."

### Questions

1. "How many trucking clients do you have? How many trucks in total?" *(Book size. A 50-truck book is worth more than 20 carrier relationships.)*
2. "When a carrier of yours has a problem with a driver citation, what happens? Do you get involved?"
3. "Which carriers do you worry about? What size are they, and what is the warning sign?"
4. "Do you currently offer, or refer out, any hours-of-service or ELD compliance help?" *(If they already refer someone out, we are a better version of an existing motion. If not, we may have to build the motion.)*
5. "What is a 'loss prevention' or safety service worth to you — what would make you spend your own money on it for a carrier?" *(Their answer defines the deal shape: co-branded report, referral fee, bundled service.)*
6. "If I gave you a free one-page HOS review of any carrier you nominate — built from their own public FMCSA record — would you send it to them?" *(This is the channel test. "Yes" means the channel is real.)*
7. "What would you need to see before you would attach your name to a recommendation? Insurance? A US entity? Someone you can call in the US?" *(This produces our actual channel requirements — likely the most valuable output of this guide.)*
8. "If one of your carriers used a service like this and it went well, what would you do about it? What would it have to do for you to recommend it to the next one?" *(The referral trigger.)*
9. "What is your commission on a trucking policy, and what would a $1,500 referral fee be worth to you?" *(Tests whether a flat fee is the right currency, or whether a percentage or a co-branded report is better.)*
10. "Is there anything about this idea that makes you uncomfortable?" *(Ask it last. It surfaces the objection we did not think of.)*

**Record:** whether they would send the free review, what they need to attach their name, and the referral economics. Also any names of trucking clients who would be good Guide-A candidates.

## Guide C — ELD reseller or installer

**Who:** a business that installs ELDs and supports small fleets, or an ELD vendor's regional reseller. Prioritise anyone with 20+ small-fleet clients.

**Why:** they hold a captive list of exactly our target segment, they already have the trust, and they need a service that is not the thing they sell.

**Opening:**
> "I'm building a staffed hours-of-service desk — driver log questions, paperwork checks, correction files — delivered from an ELD-neutral operation. I'm not reselling ELDs and not building software. I want to know whether a white-label arrangement would interest a business in your position."

### Questions

1. "How many clients do you have under 50 trucks? What ELD platforms?"
2. "When a client calls you with a hours-of-service or paperwork problem — not a broken device, a compliance problem — what do you do? Who handles it?" *(The gap we would fill.)*
3. "How often does that happen, and how long does it take you?" *(Volume signal from a second, independent source.)*
4. "Would you rather handle it yourself, refer it out, or have a partner do it under your brand?" *(Three possible answers, all workable, with different margins.)*
5. "If we did it under your brand — you own the customer, we do the work — what margin would you need to be interested? 20%? 30%?" *(Price it in their terms.)*
6. "What would you need from a partner to trust them with your clients' records?" *(Likely: US entity, E&O certificate, references, data handling terms.)*
7. "Who else in your market does something like this today?" *(Competitive reality check from the channel's point of view.)*
8. "If we asked for access to your clients' ELD data, how would that work — API, file export, read-only account? What does your platform allow?" *(Data-access reality, and it may contradict our assumption.)*
9. "Would you want a free HOS review for one of your clients to test the idea?" *(A natural pilot source.)*

**Record:** margin expectations, trust requirements, data-access mechanics, and any existing partner they already use.

## Guide D — Dispatcher or compliance administrator

**Who:** the person who actually handles log problems day to day. At a 15–75 truck fleet. Not the owner — this is a different person with different information.

**Why:** this is the only way to measure real request volume and real handling cost. It is the number our entire labour model rests on.

**Opening:**
> "I'm working out what it actually takes to keep a small fleet's hours-of-service paperwork clean, and I'm not selling you anything. I'd like to understand what your week looks like when drivers have log problems. Can I ask about a normal week?"

### Questions

1. "Describe a normal week. What time does it start, what are you working on?" *(Baseline before the exception.)*
2. "How many times a week does a driver come to you with a log problem? What are the most common three?" *(Expect: certification, shipping document number, unidentified driving time, blank graphs, break timing. Compare against our violation-code priority list.)*
3. "How long does each of those take, start to finish?" *(The unit cost. Push for minutes, not "a while.")*
4. "What do you do when the driver is asleep, or the truck is moving, and the log needs fixing?" *(The 24/7 reality.)*
5. "What do you do when a carrier asks you to change something in the log that the paperwork doesn't clearly support?" *(The most important question in this guide. Watch the reaction. Do they escalate? Do they do it? Do they ask someone else? Note the answer verbatim — it tells us how a real operation handles our Rule 2.)*
6. "Who else touches these records — the ELD vendor's support, the insurance company, an outside bookkeeper?" *(The current process map.)*
7. "What would you want to hand off, if you could hand anything off?" *(Their real pain. May not be log correction at all — which would be a very useful surprise.)*
8. "Is there anything you are asked to do that you think is a waste of your time, and you do it anyway?" *(Friction, and often where a service can be honest and win.)*
9. "If somebody did this for you, what would you still need to do yourself?" *(The irreducible core of the service.)*

**Record:** the verbatim answer to Q5, the minutes per request type, and the weekly request count. These become the staffing model and the SOP's priority order.

## Synthesis — what to do with the answers

**Immediately after each round of 5–6 conversations:**

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Owners describe the Sisic pattern (owner or office edits records to make a violation disappear) | This is the pitch. Lead with it. Rebuild the one-pager around the case. |
| Owners say the problem is paperwork, not hours | Confirms the positioning. Keep it. |
| Owners say the problem is driver behaviour and coaching | We are the wrong product for them, or the product needs a coaching module. Decide. |
| Owners say they would not pay $130 but would pay $X | Re-price to the highest number that still clears the staffing economics, and re-test. If X is below the floor, the segment is wrong. |
| Dispatchers report a different problem than we assumed (e.g. unidentified driving time dominates) | Rebuild the weekly routine around the real volume mix. |
| Agents say they would send a free review | The channel is real. Build the co-branded report and start with them. |
| Agents say they cannot attach their name without a US entity and E&O | Accelerate both. This is a hard dependency, not a nice-to-have. |
| Resellers want 30% | The white-label structure still works — 70% of a 24/7 staffed desk is a very good margin. Take it. |

**Write results into `research/RESEARCH-LOG.md` as new rows, with the interview date, the participant's fleet size, and the verbatim quote.** The interview data outranks the vendor data on every question it touches.
