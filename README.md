**Two rules that everything else serves** (see `PLAN.md` §2 and the reconciliation checklist §0):

1. **We never edit a driver's ELD.** 49 CFR 395.8(f)(2) requires that all entries relating to duty status be made by the driver; 395.30(d) expressly permits a carrier — or a system acting for one — to *propose* edits, and 395.30(d)(3) requires the driver's electronic confirmation or rejection. We propose with the source document attached; the driver confirms, enters and recertifies.
2. **We never propose a correction the documents do not support.** Disputes escalate to the carrier; requests to create or erase driving time are refused in writing and logged.

**The most important facts, all verified 2026-10-19:**

- **The product is the regulation's model.** Our workflow is 395.30(d), not a workaround around it.
- **We have no publishable track record** (the partners' 18-month record is under NDA). We sell on process proof and generate our own evidence from account one, by pulling every fleet's pre-engagement FMCSA baseline on day one.
- **$130/truck/month is ~2x the automation tier** ($25–65/truck/month published) and ~2.2x the offshore anchor (Turbo ELD, from $2/day/truck). It only defends against the staffed-HOS-desk tier. Sell to 15–75 truck fleets with a live problem; hold a 10-truck minimum; never compare on price.
- **The strongest sales asset is a criminal case.** *United States v. Sisic* (D.R.I. 2020): an 11-truck carrier's owner altered thousands of ELD entries; one driver died; 18 months, $50,000, company defunct. That is our target customer, doing what an unmanaged fleet does.
